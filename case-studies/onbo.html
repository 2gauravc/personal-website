Case study · Onbo

An agentic onboarding and KYC model for complex banking clients.

Onbo is a concept for an onboarding and KYC agent that helps banks manage registry data, document collection, risk checks, exception handling, and human review.

Problem Rigid systems Agentic KYC model Integrations Human controls Lessons learned

Problem

Onboarding is a knowledge workflow disguised as a form process.

KYC and onboarding teams must interpret client structures, gather evidence, resolve document gaps, perform risk checks, and coordinate reviews. The work changes by jurisdiction, entity type, product, risk profile, and internal policy.

Why traditional KYC systems are rigid

Static workflows struggle with exception-heavy work.

Traditional systems often encode fixed steps and status fields, but real onboarding involves judgment, missing data, document ambiguity, and back-and-forth with clients and internal teams. This creates delays and duplicated effort.

Agentic KYC model

An agent can orchestrate preparation while humans own decisions.

Onbo would help classify the client profile, identify required evidence, request documents, reconcile registry data, summarize risks, and prepare review packs. It would not replace accountable review or policy judgment.

Data and tool integrations

The system needs controlled access to trusted sources.

  • Business registries, beneficial ownership sources, and sanctions screening tools.
  • Document management, CRM, workflow, and case management systems.
  • Policy libraries, risk taxonomies, and internal control guidance.
  • Audit logs that capture source evidence, agent actions, and human decisions.

Human review and controls

Governance is part of the product design.

Human reviewers need transparent evidence, confidence indicators, clear exception queues, and the ability to override or escalate. The design goal is faster preparation with stronger traceability, not unchecked automation.

Lessons learned

KYC transformation depends on context, not just forms.

The most valuable improvements come from connecting data, policy, documents, and review logic into one working context. Agentic systems can help when they reduce ambiguity and improve the quality of human review.
